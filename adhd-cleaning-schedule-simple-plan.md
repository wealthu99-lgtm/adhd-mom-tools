## Why cleaning schedules fail with ADHD

Most cleaning schedules ask for a lot of steps, a lot of decisions and perfect consistency. That is a hard fit for a brain that struggles to start and to switch tasks. The goal is not a spotless house. It is a home that works for your family.

## Four rules that make it easier

1. **Shrink the job.** "Clean the kitchen" becomes "clear the counter" or "load the dishwasher."
2. **Use a timer.** Set ten minutes and stop when it rings. A visible end makes starting easier. Our [focus timer](/adhd-focus-timer/) works well for this.
3. **Work in zones.** Pick one zone a day, such as kitchen, bathroom or the living room, instead of the whole house.
4. **Keep a minimum version.** On bad days, do only dishes, trash and one clear surface.

## A sample week

- **Monday:** kitchen reset (counters, sink, floor)
- **Tuesday:** bathroom wipe-down
- **Wednesday:** laundry day, one load through to folded
- **Thursday:** living room and toys
- **Friday:** bedrooms, a quick tidy
- **Weekend:** one bigger job, only if you have energy

Each block is about ten to fifteen minutes. Change the days to fit your life. You can build and print your own with the free [Routine Builder](/adhd-routine-builder/).

## A five-minute closing shift

Before bed, run a quick reset: dishes in the dishwasher, wipe the counter, put things back where they live. It means tomorrow starts lighter. If the thought of it feels like too much, the [What Should I Do Now? helper](/what-should-i-do-now/) can give you one tiny first step.
