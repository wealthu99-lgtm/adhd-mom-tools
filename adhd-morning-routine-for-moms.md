## Why mornings are so hard with ADHD

Mornings ask for a lot at once: time awareness, planning, switching between tasks, and remembering everyone else's things. Those are the skills ADHD makes harder, so mornings can feel chaotic even when you care deeply about getting them right.

The fix is not more willpower. It is fewer decisions and more things you can see.

## A simple 5-step morning routine template

1. **Anchor.** Pick one thing you already do every morning, like going to the bathroom or making coffee. This is your trigger for everything else.
2. **Basics for you.** Water, any medication your doctor has prescribed, and something to eat. Put them where you will see them at the anchor.
3. **Get dressed.** Decide the night before, or keep a small "default outfit" so there is nothing to choose.
4. **Launch pad.** Bags, shoes, keys and forms live in one spot by the door.
5. **Out the door with a buffer.** Set a timer for ten minutes before you actually need to leave.

## Make it visible

A routine that lives in your head will disappear on a hard morning. Put it somewhere you can see it: on your phone, or printed on the fridge. You can build one in a few minutes with our free [Routine Builder](/adhd-routine-builder/) and print it.

## The minimum version for bad mornings

Write a second, tiny routine and keep it next to the first: dressed, fed, out the door. On the mornings everything goes wrong, you do not have to decide what to cut. The decision is already made.

## School morning tips

- Prepare the night before: bags packed, clothes out, lunch started.
- Use a timer for transitions. A [visual timer for kids](/visual-timer-for-kids/) turns "hurry up" into something they can see.
- Give each child one or two jobs, not a long list.
- If you are stuck and cannot start, try the [What Should I Do Now? helper](/what-should-i-do-now/) for one tiny step.

This post is general information, not medical advice. If mornings feel unmanageable despite your best efforts, a doctor or therapist who understands ADHD can help.
