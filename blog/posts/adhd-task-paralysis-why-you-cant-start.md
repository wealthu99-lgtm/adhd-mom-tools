## What task paralysis feels like

The dishes are in the sink. The form is due Friday. You know exactly what needs to happen, you want it done, and you still can't make your body begin. Minutes pass. The guilt grows, which makes starting even harder.

If this is you, it isn't a character flaw. It's a very common experience for people with ADHD, and many moms describe it most on days when the to-do list is already overwhelming.

## Why it happens

"Task paralysis" is a popular phrase rather than a medical term, but the pattern behind it is well recognized:

- **Task initiation is an executive function skill.** Getting started depends on the same brain systems that ADHD affects, so beginning can take far more effort than the task itself.
- **Big, vague tasks hide their first step.** "Clean the house" has no obvious starting point, so the brain stalls instead of choosing one.
- **Overwhelm and perfectionism raise the stakes.** When every task feels huge or has to be done perfectly, avoiding it can feel safer than starting.
- **Urgency works better than importance.** A deadline adds pressure that "this matters" alone often doesn't, which is why last-minute pushes happen.

## 5 small ways to break the freeze

1. **Shrink the first step.** Don't aim for "do the laundry." Aim for "put one shirt in the machine." If it feels almost silly, it's the right size.
2. **Name the very next physical action.** "Open the email" is a starting point. "Deal with the email" is not.
3. **Set a short timer.** Ten minutes, or even five, with permission to stop when it rings. Knowing there's an end makes beginning less threatening.
4. **Lower the bar on purpose.** Aim for "done badly" instead of "done well." You can always improve a started task, but you can't improve one that never begins.
5. **Add a little company or noise.** Some people find it easier to start with a friend nearby, a podcast on, or a video call running quietly in the background.

## When to ask for more help

If freezing is constant, affects your work or home life, or comes with low mood or anxiety, it's worth talking to a doctor or a therapist who knows ADHD. This post is general information, not medical advice.
