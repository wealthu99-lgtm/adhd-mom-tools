## Why most planners get abandoned

Many planners ask for too much. They have tiny boxes, long daily lists and dated pages that make you feel behind when you skip a day. For an ADHD brain, that turns the planner into another source of guilt.

## What to look for

1. **Few daily priorities.** Three is easier to hold than a long list. Our [Today's 3 Musts](/todays-3-musts/) uses one for you, one for family and one for the house.
2. **A visible week.** Seeing the whole week at a glance reduces surprises.
3. **Room to brain dump.** A page for everything in your head helps. Try our [brain dump tool](/adhd-brain-dump/) first.
4. **A minimum version.** You should be able to fill in only one box and still count it as a win.
5. **Flexibility.** Undated pages and plenty of white space forgive missed days.
6. **A format you will see.** Paper on the fridge, or a digital version on your phone.

## What to avoid

- Streaks and charts that make you feel bad when you miss a day.
- Tiny boxes that need neat handwriting.
- Planners that need more than five minutes a day.
