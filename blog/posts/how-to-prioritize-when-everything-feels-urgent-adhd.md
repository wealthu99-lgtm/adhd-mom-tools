## Why everything feels urgent

When your brain treats every task as an emergency, you either freeze or jump between tasks without finishing any. Feeling urgent and being urgent are different things. The goal is to find the difference quickly, without a complicated system.

## Four questions to find what is truly urgent

Take each item and ask:

1. **Does it have a real deadline today or tomorrow?** A form due tomorrow counts. "I should really get to this" does not.
2. **Will someone be stuck or harmed if I skip it?** A prescription running out matters more than a tidy desk.
3. **Can I make it small?** If a two-minute version exists, it can often go first.
4. **Can someone else do it?** Delegate, automate or ask for help where you can.

## Sort into four piles

- **Today:** real deadlines and things that cannot wait.
- **This week:** important, but not today.
- **Someday:** nice to do, no deadline.
- **Not mine:** worries you cannot control, or tasks that belong to someone else.

If your head is too full to sort, start with a [brain dump](/adhd-brain-dump/) first. It does the first sort for you.

## Pick your three

From the "Today" pile, choose three: one for you, one for your family, one for the house. You can do this in under a minute with the [Today's 3 Musts planner](/todays-3-musts/). Anything else is not forgotten. It is safely waiting.

## Start with the smallest one

Begin with the item that is quickest or least scary. Momentum is real, and finishing one thing makes the next easier. If you cannot start, shrink the first step and set a short timer. Our [post on task paralysis](/blog/adhd-task-paralysis-why-you-cant-start/) shows how.
