## Why choosing is harder than doing

When every task feels equally heavy, your brain cannot pick, so it does nothing. The problem is not laziness. It is that too many open tasks are crowding your head. You do not need a better system. You need fewer things in view.

## A 3-step way to pick one

1. **Write everything down.** Do not sort it. A [brain dump](/adhd-brain-dump/) is fine, even on the back of an envelope.
2. **Circle one.** Pick the task with the nearest real deadline or the biggest consequence if you skip it. If nothing stands out, circle the smallest.
3. **Shrink it to two minutes.** "Call the dentist" becomes "find the number and put it on the screen." Start that.

## Three quick examples

- **School form due tomorrow:** find the form and put it on the table. Next, fill in your name.
- **Laundry mountain:** put one load in the machine. Stop there if you need to.
- **Overdue email:** open it and type one line.

## If you still cannot choose

- Pick the smallest one.
- Ask the [What Should I Do Now? helper](/what-should-i-do-now/) to pick for you.
- Choose [three priorities for today](/todays-3-musts/) and let everything else wait.
- Set a five-minute [focus timer](/adhd-focus-timer/) and promise yourself only that.

If the freeze happens a lot, our post on [task paralysis](/blog/adhd-task-paralysis-why-you-cant-start/) goes deeper, and the guide to [prioritizing when everything feels urgent](/blog/how-to-prioritize-when-everything-feels-urgent-adhd/) has more ideas.
