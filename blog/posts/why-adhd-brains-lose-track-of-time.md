## What time blindness actually feels like

You sit down to answer "one quick email" and look up to find ninety minutes gone. Or you're sure you have plenty of time to get the kids out the door, and suddenly you're eight minutes late. This isn't about not caring — it's that the internal sense most people use to feel time ticking by is much quieter in ADHD brains.

## Why it happens

- Time estimation relies on the same executive-function systems that ADHD affects, so tracking duration takes more conscious effort than it does for neurotypical brains.
- Hyperfocus can compress or erase the sense of time passing entirely during an absorbing task.
- Without a visible or audible cue, time is easy to lose because there's no internal alarm reminding you it's moving.

## What actually helps

The fix isn't "try harder to notice the time" — it's making time impossible to ignore:

- **Visual timers** that show time draining, not just a number counting down.
- **Frequent external check-ins** — an alarm every 20-30 minutes during a task, not just one at the end.
- **Time-blocking** your day into short, visible chunks instead of one long unstructured stretch.
