## The 60-second reset

You do not need a plan right now. You need a pause. Try this:

1. **Seconds 0 to 15: breathe out slowly.** Breathe in for 4 and out for 6, three or four times. A long exhale tells your body it is not an emergency.
2. **Seconds 15 to 30: name what is in front of you.** Say it out loud or in your head: "I have dinner, a form due tomorrow, and a toddler who is crying." Naming it makes it smaller than the cloud in your head.
3. **Seconds 30 to 45: pick one thing.** Not the most important thing in your life. The thing that matters most in the next hour, or the easiest one if you cannot choose.
4. **Seconds 45 to 60: shrink it and start.** "Make dinner" becomes "put the pan on the stove." Set a five-minute timer and begin.

If you freeze at step 3, the [What Should I Do Now? helper](/what-should-i-do-now/) will pick a tiny step for you in about ten seconds.

## What to do after the reset

- **Get it out of your head.** A [brain dump](/adhd-brain-dump/) turns a swirl of worries into a short list.
- **Choose three things for today.** Use [Today's 3 Musts](/todays-3-musts/) and let everything else wait.
- **Ask for one specific thing.** "Can you take the kids for 20 minutes?" is easier to say yes to than "I need help."
- **Lower the bar.** On a hard day, doing the minimum is a good day.

For a longer guide, read [ADHD overwhelm: what it is and 7 ways to calm it](/blog/adhd-overwhelm-what-it-is-and-how-to-calm-it/).

## When it is more than overwhelm

If you feel overwhelmed most days, exhausted no matter how much you rest, or hopeless, please tell a doctor or therapist. If you are pregnant or have recently had a baby and feel this way, tell your doctor or midwife. If you might hurt yourself, call or text 988 in the US, or contact your local emergency number.
