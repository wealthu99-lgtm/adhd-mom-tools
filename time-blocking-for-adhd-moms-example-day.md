## Why broad blocks beat exact schedules

A minute-by-minute schedule falls apart the first time a child spills juice. Broad blocks, like "morning launch" or "focus block," bend without breaking. They give your day shape without demanding perfection.

## A realistic example day

Here is one example for a mom with school-age kids. Change it to fit your life.

- **7:00 to 8:30, Morning launch.** Breakfast, dressed, bags, out the door. Use a [routine](/adhd-routine-builder/) and a timer.
- **8:30 to 9:00, Reset.** Dishes, five-minute tidy, water, plan your day.
- **9:00 to 11:30, Focus block.** Work or admin. Start with a 15-minute [focus timer](/adhd-focus-timer/) and one task.
- **11:30 to 12:30, Lunch and rest.** Eat something, take a real break.
- **12:30 to 2:30, Flexible block.** Errands, appointments or a home reset.
- **2:30 to 5:30, Kids time.** Pickup, snack, homework, play.
- **5:30 to 7:00, Dinner and clean-up.**
- **7:00 to 8:30, Bedtime.** Use a visual timer for each step.
- **8:30 to 9:00, Tomorrow prep.** Ten-minute tidy, a short brain dump, choose [your 3 musts](/todays-3-musts/).

## Rules that make it work

1. **Fixed events first.** School, work and appointments go in before anything else.
2. **Three to six blocks a day.** More than that and the plan becomes another source of overwhelm.
3. **Name flexible blocks by type.** "Admin" is easier to meet than "call the insurance company at 10:15."
4. **Add buffers.** Transitions take longer than you think.
5. **Swap, do not stack.** If something new appears, move something out.

## When the plan falls apart

It will. When it does, ask one question: what is the single most important block left today? Do that one, and move the rest.
