## What a visual timer is

Most timers show numbers. A visual timer shows a shape, such as a coloured wedge or ring, that gets smaller as time passes. You do not need to read or calculate anything. You just see how much is left.

## Why it helps with ADHD

Many people with ADHD find it hard to sense how much time has passed. Twenty minutes can feel like five, or two hours can vanish. A visual timer replaces that internal sense with something external you can check at a glance. It also gives you a clear start and a clear end, which can make starting less intimidating.

## Types of visual timers

- **Sand timers:** simple and screen-free, but fixed to one length.
- **Disc or wedge timers:** a colour shrinks as time runs down, flexible lengths, usually battery powered.
- **Online or app timers:** adjustable, with sounds and logs. Our free [focus timer](/adhd-focus-timer/) offers a ring or pie view and works offline once added to your home screen.

## 6 ways to use a visual timer

1. **Start tiny.** Set 10 minutes and promise yourself only that.
2. **Name the task.** Writing what you are doing keeps you on track.
3. **Use it for transitions.** Leaving the house, ending screen time, starting dinner.
4. **Tackle the task you avoid.** A timer makes a dreaded job feel finite.
5. **Plan the stop.** If you hyperfocus, set a timer so you do not miss pickup or meals.
6. **Take real breaks.** Stand up, drink water, look away from the screen.

## Common mistakes

- Setting it too long, so it feels like a burden.
- Using it as a punishment instead of a support.
- Skipping the break and burning out.
